ZORK I

The Great Underground Empire


1. THE WHITE HOUSE

I was standing in an open field west of a white house with a boarded front door. There was a small
mailbox. There always is.

I walked round the north side, where the windows were all boarded up, to the back, where a small
window stood slightly ajar. But first I went into the forest, because a narrow path wound north
into the trees, and I wanted to know where it went. It went to one particularly large tree with low
branches, and ten feet up, in a small bird's nest, lay an egg encrusted with precious jewels,
inlaid with gold, lapis lazuli and mother-of-pearl. Some childless songbird had scavenged it from
somewhere. It was hinged and closed with a delicate clasp, and it looked very fragile. I took it.
Somewhere in the distance the songbird chirped.

Back at the house I pushed the window open and climbed into the kitchen. There was a bottle of
water on the table and a brown sack, which held a lunch and a clove of garlic. I took the garlic
and left the lunch. In the living room I found a brass lantern, an elvish sword hung over a trophy
case with a glass front, and a large oriental rug. In the attic, by the light of the lantern, I
found a coil of rope and a nasty-looking knife.

Under the rug was a trap door. I opened it, and a rickety staircase led down into the dark. The
moment I reached the bottom, the trap door crashed shut over my head, and I heard someone barring
it.

There was only one way to go, and that was on.


2. THE TROLL

The cellar was dark and damp. My sword glowed with a faint blue light, which I later learned meant
that something nasty was close by. The passage north led to a small room with bloodstains and deep
scratches on the walls, and in it a troll, brandishing a bloody axe, blocked every exit.

The sword was glowing very brightly now. The troll swung his axe, and it nicked my arm as I dodged.
I swung back. The haft of my sword knocked him out cold, and the unconscious troll could not defend
himself. He died. Almost as soon as he breathed his last, a cloud of sinister black fog wrapped
round him, and when it lifted the body was gone.

I put the sword down there. I'd had enough of it.

East of the cellar, at the edge of a chasm too deep to see the bottom of, was an art gallery.
Vandals of exceptional taste had stolen most of the paintings, but they had left one, a painting of
unparalleled beauty, and I became a vandal myself.

West of the troll's room began a maze of twisty little passages, all alike. Somewhere in the middle
lay the skeleton of a luckless adventurer, with his rusty knife, his useless lantern, a skeleton
key, and an old leather bag bulging with coins. I took the coins. He wasn't going to need them.

The far side of the maze opened into a room where a cyclops blocked a staircase. He looked prepared
to eat horses, let alone adventurers, and the bloodstains on the walls suggested he usually did.

My arm was still bleeding from the troll, and I wasn't going to fight anything. So I said the name
of his father's deadly enemy.

"Ulysses."

The cyclops fled in terror, smashing a hole through the east wall of the room as he went. That hole
came out in my own living room, through a wooden door I hadn't been able to open. It made going
home a great deal easier.


3. THE THIEF

I sat in the cyclops's room until my wound healed. Then I climbed the staircase.

At the top was a treasure room with walls of solid granite, the floor scattered with old bags that
crumbled when I touched them. A silver chalice stood in the corner. Leaning against the wall was a
suspicious-looking man with a large bag and a deadly stiletto. He was the thief, and I had been
told about him. Before I could say anything, the stiletto touched my forehead and blood ran into my
eyes.

I held out the jewelled egg. He was taken aback by my generosity, accepted the egg, and stopped to
admire it.

Then I attacked him with the knife from the attic. A quick stroke, but he was on guard. I parried a
lightning thrust, and he saluted me with a grim nod. My knife missed him by an inch. His stiletto
missed me. Then he stumbled, and I knocked him into dreamland, and the unconscious thief could not
defend himself. The black fog came for him as it had for the troll.

On the floor was the egg, opened now, because the thief had the skill to open it and I did not.
Nestled inside was a golden clockwork canary with ruby eyes and a silver beak. I took the chalice,
went down the stairs and through the cyclops's hole, and put my first treasures in the trophy case:
the painting, the chalice and the coins. Then I went back for the egg.

In the forest I wound up the canary. It chirped an aria from a forgotten opera, slightly off-key,
and a real songbird flew down to a branch over my head and opened its beak to sing. A beautiful
brass bauble dropped out of it, bounced off my head and landed glimmering in the grass. The canary
ran down, and the songbird flew away.

The egg, the canary and the bauble all went into the case.


4. THE TEMPLE AND THE RAINBOW

The trap door was still open from the living room side. Past the troll's room and a circular room
full of cave-ins was a dome, ringed by a wooden railing high above a room I couldn't see. I tied
the rope to the railing and climbed down. On a white marble pedestal sat a flaming torch made of
ivory. I took it, and since it gave all the light I needed, I put out my lantern.

South was a temple, with an ancient prayer carved on the east wall and a brass bell on the floor.
East of that, in a room like an Egyptian tomb, was the solid gold coffin of Ramses II. It was too
heavy to carry with everything else, so I left the lantern and the garlic in the temple. Inside the
coffin lay a sceptre, possibly that of ancient Egypt itself, ornamented in coloured enamel and
tapering to a sharp point.

At the south end of the temple stood an altar with a candle burning at each end and a large black
book open to page 569. I prayed.

I was in a forest, with the coffin in my arms and the sun overhead.

From the top of the Great Canyon I could see the Frigid River coming out of its dark cavern,
Aragain Falls with its rainbow, and the White Cliffs. I climbed down to the canyon bottom and
walked north to the end of the rainbow, a little rocky beach where sunlight came in from above.
There I took the sceptre out of the coffin and waved it.

The rainbow became solid and, I venture to say, walkable. The giveaway was the stairs and the
bannister. And at the end of the rainbow, where it ought to be, a shimmering pot of gold appeared.

The coffin, the sceptre and the gold went into the case.


5. THE DAM

The torch led me east again, to a room so deafeningly loud that I could hardly think. I shouted
"Echo" at it. The acoustics changed subtly and the room fell eerily quiet, and on the floor lay a
large platinum bar.

Up a stairway was Flood Control Dam #3, once quite a tourist attraction, now abandoned, with water
pouring over the top. In the lobby someone had left a matchbook that said "Visit Beautiful FCD#3".
In the ransacked maintenance room I found a wrench and a screwdriver. I pushed the yellow button,
and the green bubble on the dam's control panel began to glow. I turned the bolt with the wrench,
and the sluice gates opened.

The reservoir behind the dam drained away to a pile of mud, with an old trunk bulging with jewels
lying half buried in it. On the far shore was a hand-held air pump.

I went back down through the dome, past my lantern in the temple, prayed at the altar again, and
walked home with the bar and the trunk.


6. THE LAND OF THE DEAD

This time I collected everything from the temple: the lantern, the garlic, the brass bell, the
black book and the pair of candles. I blew out the candles and went down into the dark, and down
again, to a gateway inscribed "Abandon every hope all ye who enter here!"

Beyond it lay a desolation and a pile of mangled bodies, and I could hear thousands of voices
lamenting some hideous fate. Evil spirits barred the way and jeered at me.

I rang the bell. It turned red hot and fell from my hand, and the wraiths, as if paralysed, stopped
jeering and slowly turned to face me. In my confusion I dropped the candles too. I picked them up,
struck one of the matches, and lit them. The flames flickered wildly and danced, the earth trembled
under my feet, and the spirits cowered. Then I read the prayer from the book. As the last word
faded, a loud, commanding voice said, "Begone, fiends!" There was a heart-stopping scream, and the
spirits fled through the walls.

The Land of the Living Dead was full of weeping, and of the stacked remains of dozens of
adventurers less fortunate than me. In one corner was a beautifully carved crystal skull, grinning
at me rather nastily. I took it anyway.


7. DOWN THE RIVER

At the foot of the dam lay a pile of plastic. I pumped it up into a small boat, climbed in, and
launched it on the Frigid River.

The river carried me past the White Cliffs and into a valley, and the rumbling ahead grew louder.
When a red buoy came bobbing by, I grabbed it, steered for a sandy beach on the east shore, and
climbed out. The rumbling was Aragain Falls. Another few minutes and I would have gone over.

Inside the buoy was a large emerald. A shovel lay on the beach. In a sand-filled cave behind it I
dug, and dug, and dug, and on the fourth try uncovered a jewelled scarab.

From there I walked up to the top of the falls and straight across the rainbow I had made solid.
Then home, with the skull, the emerald and the scarab.


8. THE COAL MINE

Five treasures left.

Through the reservoir, now only a stream, was the Atlantis Room, long under water, where Poseidon's
own crystal trident lay on the shore. Beyond it, past a mirror room and a cold passage and a slide,
was the entrance to an old mine. To the north was a room with a large vampire bat on the ceiling,
who was obviously deranged, and holding his nose because of the garlic in my hand. Beside him was
an exquisite jade figurine.

In the shaft room next door a basket hung on a chain over a shaft. I lit my lantern and put the
ivory torch in the basket. A naked flame was about to become a very bad idea. Below was a room that
smelled strongly of coal gas, with a sapphire-encrusted bracelet on the floor. Beyond it was a coal
mine of nondescript passages, a rickety ladder, and at the bottom a dead end with a small pile of
coal. I carried the coal all the way back up and lowered it down the shaft in the basket.

Then down again by the ladder to a room cluttered with broken timbers, where the passage west
narrowed to almost nothing. I put everything down to squeeze through. On the far side was the
bottom of the shaft, with my basket waiting, and south of that a cold room with a machine like a
clothes dryer, with a switch labelled START that no human finger could turn.

The screwdriver could. I put the coal inside, closed the lid, and turned the switch. The machine
came to life with a dazzling display of coloured lights and bizarre noises. When it calmed down, I
opened the lid on a huge diamond.

The diamond and the torch went up in the basket. I squeezed back, picked up my things, climbed out
through the coal mine, and hauled the basket up. I took the figurine from under the bat's nose and
slid down the long slide to the cellar.

The trident, the bracelet, the diamond, the torch and the figurine went into the case. Nineteen
treasures.

An almost inaudible voice whispered in my ear: "Look to your treasures for the final secret."


9. THE BARROW

In the trophy case, among the treasures, was a map that hadn't been there before. It showed a
forest with three clearings and a house in the largest of them. One of the paths leaving that
clearing led southwest and was marked "To Stone Barrow".

West of the house, by the little mailbox where I had started, a secret path now led southwest into
the forest. At its end stood a massive barrow of stone with a huge open door. I went in, and the
door closed behind me.

Ahead was an enormous cavern, brightly lit, with a wide stream running through it and a small
wooden footbridge over the stream. Beyond the bridge a path led into a dark tunnel. Above the
bridge, floating in the air, was a sign.

"All ye who stand before this bridge have completed a great and perilous adventure which has tested
your wit and courage. You have mastered the first part of the ZORK trilogy. Those who pass over
this bridge must be prepared to undertake an even greater adventure that will severely test your
skill and bravery!"

Three hundred and fifty points. Master Adventurer.

I looked at the bridge for a long time. Then I crossed it.
