ADVENTURE

Down the Colossal Cave


1. THE WELL HOUSE

The road ended at a small brick building, with forest all around and a stream running out of the
building and away down a gully. That was all there was: the end of a road, a building, a stream. I
have never been anywhere that felt so much like the start of something.

The building turned out to be a well house built over a large spring. Somebody had left supplies on
the floor as if they had been expecting me: a set of keys, some tasty food, a shiny brass lamp and
an empty bottle. I took the lamp and the keys and went looking for wherever the stream went.

It went south, down a valley beside a rocky bed, until the whole stream splashed into a two-inch
slit in the rock and disappeared. Beyond that the streambed was dry, and a little further on, in a
twenty-foot depression floored with dirt, I found a strong steel grate set in concrete. The keys
fitted. The grate swung open on a small chamber, and a low crawl over cobbles led west into the
dark.

I lit the lamp before I went in. I'm glad I did.

In the cobble crawl someone had thrown away a small wicker cage, and I picked it up for no better
reason than that it was there. Beyond it was a room full of debris washed in from the surface, and
on its wall someone had written, "Magic word XYZZY." A three-foot black rod with a rusty star on
one end lay among the mud. I left it where it was.

An awkward canyon led up and west into a splendid chamber thirty feet high, its walls like frozen
rivers of orange stone. A cheerful little bird sat there singing. It went into the wicker cage
without much fuss, and I carried it on, past a small pit breathing white mist, and down a flight of
rough stone steps.

At the bottom was the Hall of Mists, a vast hall stretching out of sight to the west, with wisps of
white mist swaying to and fro almost as if alive. A cold wind blew up the wide stone staircase.
That was the moment I knew I was really in the cave.


2. THE FIRST LOAD

South of the hall, in a low room, a crude note on the wall said, "You won't get it up the steps."
Below it lay a large nugget of gold. I took it anyway.

That was where the first dwarf found me. A threatening little dwarf came out of the shadows and
followed me back into the hall and on to the Hall of the Mountain King, where a huge green fierce
snake barred the way. There the dwarf threw a nasty little axe at me, missed, cursed, and ran away.

I let the bird out of the cage. It flew at the snake in an astounding flurry and drove it right
away. I have never seen anything like it, and I doubt the snake had either.

The axe lay on the floor where the dwarf had dropped it. I picked it up. Then I collected what the
hall had to offer: precious jewellery in the south side chamber, rare coins in the west side
chamber, and bars of silver in a low passage to the north. A second dwarf followed me about,
throwing knives that missed. I threw the axe at him. That missed too, and he got tired of the game
and slipped away.

North of the silver was a large room with a big "Y2" painted on a rock. As I came in, a hollow
voice said, "Plugh."

Another dwarf came out of the shadows. I said the word back to the voice, and the cave let go of
me. I was standing in the well house with my arms full of treasure. I put it all down on the floor,
and the floor seemed to approve.


3. WATER AND OIL

I filled the bottle at the spring, took the food, said "Plugh," and was back at Y2, where the dwarf
was waiting. The first axe missed. The second one didn't. The little dwarf vanished in a cloud of
greasy black smoke.

South and down from Y2 the passages grew strange. There was a dusty room with a big hole in its
floor, a complex junction where three passages met, and then Bedquilt, a long east-west passage
with holes everywhere. Another dwarf came at me in the dusty room, and the axe found him first.

West of Bedquilt was a room whose walls looked like Swiss cheese, and west of that the Twopit Room,
with a pit at each end. At the bottom of the western pit was a tiny little plant murmuring "Water,
water..." I gave it my bottle of water. It spurted into furious growth for a few seconds and became
a twelve-foot beanstalk, bellowing "Water!! Water!!"

The nearest water was a long way off. I went up through the Slab Room, where an immense slab had
fallen from the ceiling, into a secret canyon, then north past an enormous two-sided mirror hanging
in mid-air, which is there, they say, for the use of the dwarves, who are extremely vain. Beyond it
lay an underground reservoir under a cloud of rising mist. I filled the bottle and walked all the
way back.

The second watering did it. The plant grew explosively until a gigantic beanstalk reached all the
way up to a hole high in the wall.

There was a pool of oil in the eastern pit. I filled the bottle with that, and then I climbed the
beanstalk.


4. THE GIANT, THE PIRATE AND THE TROLL

The hole led into a long, narrow corridor, and the corridor into the Giant Room. The ceiling was
too high for my lamp to find. On the west wall someone had scrawled "Fee fie foe foo" [sic], and on
the floor sat a large nest full of golden eggs.

North of it, a massive, rusty iron door barred the way. I poured the oil on its hinges.

Out from the shadows behind me pounced a bearded pirate. "Har, har," he chortled. "I'll just take
all this booty and hide it away with me chest deep in the maze!" And he snatched my golden eggs and
vanished into the gloom.

The door groaned open onto a magnificent cavern where a stream cascaded over a sparkling waterfall
into a roaring whirlpool. A jewel-encrusted trident lay at the edge. From there a steep incline
dropped into a large, low room, the sort of place you can climb down into but not back out of. A
winding corridor led up to a chasm full of white mist, and across the mist went a rickety wooden
bridge with a sign: "Stop! Pay troll!"

The troll stepped out from under the bridge, and I had nothing to give him but the trident, which I
was going to need.

So I said the words on the giant's wall, standing at the edge of that chasm: "Fee. Fie. Foe. Foo."
The cave answered, "Done!" Far away, I hoped, the eggs had gone home to their nest.

Then I walked back for them, the long way round, since there is no climbing back up that incline.
Down to the low room, through an Oriental Room where a delicate Ming vase sat waiting for a later
trip, back through the Swiss cheese room and the Twopit Room, up the beanstalk again, and there was
the nest of golden eggs in the Giant Room, as if they had never left. I carried them all the way
back to the chasm and gave them to the troll. He caught them and scurried out of sight. Then I
crossed.


5. THE BEAR

On the far side, a corridor led to a fork. The left fork rumbled, and the right fork sloped gently
down a passage of limestone to a room with a sign over the entrance: "Caution! Bear in room!"

At the far end of the barren room, held by a solid gold chain, was a ferocious cave bear. I gave
him my food. He wolfed it down and calmed right down, and became, of all things, friendly. The keys
from the well house unlocked his chain. I took the chain, which was a treasure in its own right,
and the bear followed me about like a large shaggy dog.

The left fork led past walls so warm they were almost hot, with a roar from the north that made the
whole cave tremble, to a stifling little chamber full of boulders. There I found a selection of
rare spices.

Back at the chasm, the troll stepped out to demand his toll again. I let the bear go. He lumbered
toward the troll, who let out a startled shriek and fled. The bear gave up the chase and wandered
back, and I crossed the bridge for nothing and left the bear sitting on the far side, looking
pleased with himself.


6. THE OYSTER

North of the complex junction, up a low hands-and-knees passage, was a room carved out of
sedimentary rock, its walls full of fossil shells, and in it was an enormous clam with its shell
tightly closed. I prised it open with the trident. A glistening pearl fell out and rolled away down
a ragged corridor. "Goodness," the cave remarked, "this must really be an oyster." I found the
pearl in a little cul-de-sac at the bottom, and went home by way of Y2.

The well house floor was getting crowded: gold, silver, jewellery, coins, chain, spices, trident
and pearl.


7. THE PLOVER ROOM AND THE DRAGON

North of the Oriental Room was a misty cavern, and west of that an alcove, where an extremely tight
tunnel led east toward an eerie light. It was far too tight to squeeze through with my arms full,
so I set down the lamp and the axe and squeezed through. On the other side was a small chamber lit
by an eerie green light, and an emerald the size of a plover's egg. I squeezed back with it, picked
up my things, and collected the Ming vase from the Oriental Room and a small velvet pillow from a
Soft Room nearby, a quiet place hung with curtains and carpeted thick.

Then up the beanstalk once more. The troll had my golden eggs, and the giant's words don't care who
has them. I said them again in the Giant Room, and a large nest full of golden eggs appeared out of
nowhere.

In a secret canyon, a huge green fierce dragon lay sprawled on a Persian rug. I asked myself how to
kill a dragon, and the cave asked me back: "With what? Your bare hands?" I said yes.

"Congratulations! You have just vanquished a dragon with your bare hands! (Unbelievable, isn't it?)"

It is unbelievable, but I had the rug under my arm to prove it.

Back in the well house, I put the pillow down first and laid the vase gently on it. Then I went
back to Y2 and said a word I had not known that morning: "Plover." It took me straight to the
green-lit chamber, and northeast of it was a room as dark as a cellar, where a stone tablet read,
"Congratulations on bringing light into the dark-room!" A platinum pyramid stood there, eight
inches on a side. One "plover" and one "plugh" later, it was on the well house floor with the rest.


8. THE MAZE

Two treasures were missing: whatever lay beyond the fissure in the Hall of Mists, and whatever the
pirate had in his chest.

I said "xyzzy" in the well house and found myself in the debris room beside the black rod. This
time I took it. At the east bank of the fissure, which was too wide to jump, I waved the rod, and a
crystal bridge sprang across the gap. On the far side lay a heap of diamonds.

West of there was the maze: twisty little passages, all alike. I won't describe them, because there
is nothing to describe. South, east, south, south, south, north, east, to the brink of a
thirty-foot pit with a massive orange column down one wall, then north, east and northwest, and
there it was, the pirate's dead end, and the pirate's treasure chest.

The moment I picked it up, a sepulchral voice reverberated through the whole cave: "Cave closing
soon. All adventurers exit immediately through main office."

I climbed down the orange column, which brought me out in the chamber where I had caught the bird
all that time ago. Two rooms east was the debris room, and one "xyzzy" later I was setting down the
chest, the diamonds and the rod with everything else. Fifteen treasures lay on the floor of the
well house.

There was one small thing left to do. In an anteroom beyond the complex junction, under a sign
reading "Cave under construction beyond this point. Proceed at own risk. [Witt Construction
Company]", lay a few recent issues of a magazine called Spelunker Today. I carried them east to
Witt's End, where passages lead off in all directions, and left them there.

"You really are at wit's end," the cave said, and gave me a point for it.


9. THE REPOSITORY

I waited. On the sixth turn the voice intoned: "The cave is now closed." There was a blinding flash
of light and a small puff of orange smoke.

When my eyes came back, I was at the northeast end of an immense room, larger even than the giant
room, lit by massive torches far overhead. It was a repository for the Adventure itself. There were
piles of empty bottles, a nursery of young beanstalks murmuring quietly, a bed of oysters, a bundle
of black rods with rusty stars, and a collection of brass lanterns. Off to one side, a great many
dwarves were sleeping on the floor, snoring loudly. A sign said, "Do not disturb the dwarves!" An
immense mirror ran the length of the room.

At the southwest end was a pit full of fierce green snakes, a row of wicker cages each holding a
sulking little bird, a scattering of velvet pillows, and a bundle of black rods with rusty marks on
their ends. Not stars. Marks. Beside a steel grate, a sign read, "TREASURE VAULT. Keys in main
office."

I took one of the rods with the rusty mark, carried it to the northeast end, set it down among the
sleeping dwarves, and walked back to the southwest end. Then I said the word.

"Blast."

There was a loud explosion, and a twenty-foot hole appeared in the far wall, burying the dwarves in
the rubble. I marched through the hole and found myself in the main office, where a cheering band
of friendly elves carried the conquering adventurer off into the sunset.

Three hundred and fifty points out of three hundred and fifty. Grandmaster Adventurer.

I still think about the bird sometimes, sulking in a wicker cage at the bottom of the world. And
about the bear, sitting happily on the far side of a chasm, who never asked for anything but lunch.
