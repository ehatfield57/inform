WISHBRINGER
The longest delivery of my life

I was in the middle of fighting a dragon when Mr. Crisp started yelling.

It was a good daydream. I had a Magick sword and a princess in a tower and a reptile the size of a
barn breathing fire at me, and I was just raising the blade to strike when a voice from the Post
Office below shattered the whole thing like a dropped teacup. I was sitting on the hilltop above
Festeron, where I always sit when I'm supposed to be sorting mail.

"Where have you been?" Mr. Crisp barked as I came in. He was behind the counter, hiding a stack of
other people's postcards. "Daydreaming again, eh?"

He tossed a mysterious envelope onto the counter. Special Delivery, he said, for the Magick Shoppe
up on the cliffs north of town. "That means NOW! The Magick Shoppe closes at five o'clock!"

When I stepped outside, somebody slammed the door and locked it behind me.

---

I took the long way, through the cemetery, because I'm the kind of person who takes the long way.
The old gravedigger was resting under a willow beside a fresh grave. He looked at the address on my
envelope and frowned. "Could've sworn I buried that old woman years ago," he said. Then he picked
up his shovel and wandered off, and locked the cemetery gate behind him, and told me with a wink to
keep out after Dark. I noticed he said it with a capital D.

There was an old bone at the bottom of the grave. I took it. I don't know why. Maybe I already had
a feeling about Miss Voss's poodle.

The poodle was the tiniest dog I've ever seen, and the angriest. It came charging across the road
outside the librarian's cottage, yapping, showing its little teeth. I gave it the bone. It lay down
and gnawed, and kept one red eye on me the whole time.

Miss Voss herself caught me outside the library. "Be a sweetie and give this note to your dear boss,
Mr. Crisp," she cooed, and pressed a violet slip of paper into my hand. "Toody-loo!" I put it in my
pocket. I had no intention of being anybody's sweetie, but you don't argue with the town librarian.

There was a gold coin glinting in the fountain in the park. I'm a mail carrier, not a saint.

The trail up the cliff winds back and forth like it's trying to lose you. At the top, I could see the
shadow of Post Office Hill creeping over the village as the sun went down, and a tiny figure came out
of the Post Office, looked straight at me, checked its watch, and shook its fist.

---

The Magick Shoppe was straight out of a fairy tale: painted shutters, thatched roof, a little bell
over the door. Inside, a grandfather clock ticked too loudly, and then the curtain at the back
opened so fast I jumped.

The woman who came out was older than my oldest aunt. Her face was thin and pale, like a fading
signature in an antique book, but her eyes remembered everything they had ever seen.

She couldn't find her glasses, so I read her the letter.

"Kidnapped," she whispered, when I'd finished. Her cat, Chaos, had been taken by someone she called
the Evil One, and the ransom was a thing called the Stone of Dreams, which she had spent her whole
life hiding. "My youth, my home and family, all were forfeited for its protection. And now it claims
my only companion."

She gave me a tip, which I'm not supposed to accept: a little metal can off a shelf of cheap gags.
Then she walked me to the door, and touched the middle of my forehead with one finger, and told me
to look out for a black cat with one white spot, right there.

"Return the cat to me," she said, "and Wishbringer shall be yours."

The door closed. The bell tinkled. And Festeron was gone.

The fog had swallowed the whole village. Only the top of Post Office Hill rose above it, and on top
of the hill, where the Post Office used to be, there was a tower.

---

The fog lifted at the bottom of the cliff, and the town underneath it was called Witchville now.
Everything was the same and everything was wrong. A vulture watched me from a dead tree. When I
pulled the dead branch off, the vulture flew away in a huff, which was the only time all night
anything was afraid of me.

A troll had built a toll gate across the bridge. I didn't have a toll, but I had the old woman's
little can, so I opened it, and a real live rattlesnake leaped out. The troll screamed and ran
across the bridge and I never saw it again.

Something rattled in the empty can. I squeezed it until the false bottom popped, and a small stone
dropped out into my hand and began to glow with a violet-white light.

That was Wishbringer. I didn't know it yet. I just knew I could see in the dark now.

---

There was a platypus stuck in a pit by the lake, of all things. I lowered the branch in and it
grabbed on and I pulled it out, and it waddled around on the sand, drew an X with its tail, gave me a
very dignified nod and swam away. I dug at the X and found a silver whistle.

When I blew it, the wind picked me up and carried me across the water to Misty Island and a castle
full of platypuses drinking tea. The snow-white one on the throne was King Anatinus, and the one I'd
rescued was his daughter, Princess Tasmania. He gave me a little wizard's hat and told me to take it
to the sea. "There you will find a creature learned in the lore of Magick. Heed him well!"

You'd be surprised how many things in this story are platypuses.

---

The cemetery was worse at night. Ribbons of luminous mist darted between the tombstones, and then
they gathered into a cloud of eldritch vapors and circled me like sharks, whispering things. I
jumped into the open grave, and they moaned with disappointment and couldn't follow.

There was a hole in the side of the grave. Tunnels. And at the top of one of those tunnels, under a
bunk I had to shove aside from below, a jail cell with a thick blanket in it. I took the blanket.
Everything I owned had to go down the hole ahead of me before I could squeeze back through.

The next tunnel ended in a grue's nest.

I want to be very clear: this is not a thing a person should walk into. There were piles of bones
and rusty elvish swords everywhere, and in one corner, curled up, a little grue, snoring fitfully,
turning away from my glowing stone. It was starting to stir. I tucked the blanket over it. It sighed
and lay still.

There was a refrigerator in the nest. I don't know why a grue has a refrigerator. Inside was a bottle
and an earthworm, and I took the earthworm, and I tiptoed out.

---

The creature learned in the lore of Magick turned out to be a pelican sitting on a tiny lighthouse at
the mouth of the river. It sniffed the wizard's hat, and put it on, and gave me a sly wink, and the
lighthouse blazed to life. Its beam wrote a single word across a passing cloud:

KALUZE.

Then the pelican flew away, wearing the hat, and a voice in my head said, "Good luck, brave
Adventurer!"

---

Miss Voss, the former town librarian, was selling tickets at the movie theater. I bought one with my
gold coin. And then the night filled with rhythmic thunder, and a platoon of gigantic leather army
boots marched around the corner.

The Boot Patrol tied me up and carried me, kicking and screaming, in an especially smelly boot to the
police station, where Sergeant MacGuffin looked at me and asked what I was. "A nightcrawler," said
the Tall Boot. "Out after curfew." The Sergeant said the Tower wanted all prisoners held for
questioning. "Cell Three."

It was the same cell, with the same hole in the floor I'd come up through an hour before. I was out
of there before they finished locking the door.

They'd taken my ticket, though. I had to buy another, and this time I went straight in.

---

The gravedigger was taking tickets. The theater inside was spotless and alien, the seats deep and
soft, the picture a blur until I found a pair of old 3D glasses under a seat and put them on.

The movie was about an old woman squinting through a telescope in a laboratory full of bubbling
chemicals. A vulture landed on her shoulder and whispered in her ear. She swung the telescope around,
and the camera looked through it, and what it was looking at was a little movie theater exactly like
the one I was sitting in. Miss Voss waved timidly at the camera. In the corner of the lab, for just a
moment, I saw a black cat asleep.

Then the old woman pulled a switch labeled "Palace Theater," and the screen went black.

---

I fed the earthworm to the piranha in the park fountain, which is a sentence I never expected to
write, and while it was eating I took the brass token from the bottom. In the video arcade by the
wharf, stunted humanoids crowded every machine but one. I put my token in. I moved the blinking star
two squares left and two squares down. The big red button asked me if I was sure. Then it asked me
again.

I pressed it anyway, and the machine took me apart one molecule at a time and broadcast me back to
the Hilltop, where the tower stood in bleak silhouette against the moon.

"Kaluze," I said, and the drawbridge came down.

---

Princess Tasmania was chained to the floor of the vestibule. "Behind you!" she cried. "It's a trap!"
Too late. The drawbridge crashed shut, and I turned to face my enemy, and it was... Mr. Crisp.

"Nice of you to drop by," he sneered. "Saves me the bother of tracking you down."

He had me chained in the corner of a torture chamber and was dusting off his instruments when I
remembered the violet note. I held it out to him. He went pale as he read it. Then an unbearably
sweet expression spread across his face, and he threw his lab coat in the corner, struggled into a
hideous velvet blazer, combed his hair, and ran up the ladder like a schoolboy. "Violet scolds me
when I'm late!"

There was a key in the lab coat. I got it with my fingertips. I unlocked my chains and pulled the
lever that freed the princess, and she licked my shoe and vanished in a whirlpool of light. On the
floor lay the note, which read:

    Corky-Poo, I've got a plate of fresh oatmeal cookies waiting for you. If my little poodle yaps at
    you, just say ALEXIS, HEEL and she'll behave. Violet.

---

At the top of the tower everything was blurry, until I put the 3D glasses on and the laboratory from
the movie snapped into focus around me. There was a broom. There was a control panel with a switch
marked "Security," which I turned off. And in the corner, sound asleep, there was a black cat, and I
didn't touch it, because by then I'd stopped believing anything in Witchville was what it looked like.

Behind one of the blackened paintings downstairs was a crank that lowered the drawbridge. I walked
out of the tower a free man.

The tiny poodle outside Miss Voss's cottage was a hellhound now, roaring, with bloody fangs. "Alexis,"
I said, "heel." She sat down at my feet, licked my hand and thumped her enormous tail.

The steel key on the bookcase in the cottage opened the library. The door slammed and locked behind
me, of course. In the museum at the back was a glass case, and in the case was a black marble
sculpture of a cat, with a round, shallow hole in the middle of its forehead.

I broke the glass with the broom.

---

As I lifted Wishbringer toward the sculpture, a familiar voice said, "Wait!" The old woman from the
Magick Shoppe stood in the dark of the corridor. The sculpture was Chaos, she said, the cat that was
stolen. "Now give Wishbringer to me, and together we shall rejoice."

I thought about it. I thought about the cat in the lab, and the old woman in the movie, and a woman
who couldn't find her glasses. And I kept going.

"Don't!" she barked. "No!"

Violet light poured out of the heart of the stone and lit the room like day, and the disguise burned
away, and it wasn't the old woman at all. It was her sister, the Evil One, frozen in a wail of
despair as she faded out of the world. The sculpture softened in the radiance and wriggled and leaped
across the floor, and the whole library folded shut around me like a great book closing.

---

I was standing on the cliff outside the Magick Shoppe. The sun was coming up, friendly and orange. The
Post Office was back on its hill where it belonged. And a black cat with one white spot on its
forehead was rubbing against my leg.

I knocked. The old woman came to the door in her nightgown, blinking, and the cat leaped into her arms,
and she laughed and cried at the same time.

She'd lied to me, she admitted. She'd promised me Wishbringer knowing that if I succeeded, its Magick
would be spent. "In truth, the Stone would make a poor reward," she said, tickling the cat's white
forehead. "It brings more joy in the shape of a companion than in any other. This is Wishbringer's
finest Magick."

Then she touched the violet note, still in my pocket. "Make sure you give this to Mr. Crisp when you
see him," she said, with a sly wink. "And tell him I said hello."

I'm going to be very late for work.
