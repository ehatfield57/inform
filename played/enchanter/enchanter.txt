ENCHANTER
The apprentice who was Sent

They chose me because I was nobody.

I didn't understand that at first. I was standing in the small dark chamber of the Circle, still
blinking from the spell that had fetched me there, and Belboz the Necromancer, the Eldest of the
Circle, was looking at me the way you look at a tool you aren't sure will hold. The other
Enchanters sat around him in their robes, the most powerful wizards in the land, and every one of
them looked worried.

"Krill's evil must be unmade," Belboz said. "But to send a powerful Enchanter is ill-omened. It
would be ruinous to reveal oversoon our full powers."

So they would send someone with no powers worth revealing. A novice with four spells in his book.
Me.

He gave me an ancient document that said, as far as I could make out, that this was the way it had
to be, written by a hand far wiser than his. Then the Circle rose and wove a spell together, and
the dark chamber filled with warmth, and there was a surge of power, and I was Sent.

---

I landed at a fork in the road, at dawn, with the Lonely Mountain looming over me and nothing in my
hands but my spell book.

My spell book, at that point, contained exactly four spells. Gnusto, which copies a magic scroll
into a spell book. Blorb, which protects a small object. Nitfol, which lets you talk to animals.
And frotz, which makes a thing glow. With these four, I was to overthrow the most dangerous warlock
in the world. I want that to be clear from the start, so that nobody thinks I was brave. I just
didn't have time to be anything else.

The first thing I learned on my quest was that a hero needs lunch. There was a deserted shack up
the road, all broken windows and overturned furniture, and in the corner lay an empty jug, and
behind the table a battered old lantern, and in the oven, still warm somehow, a loaf of bread. I
took all three. I filled the jug at a brook called the Shady Brook, beside the ruins of the Old
Lingolf House, and I can tell you now that I would walk back to that brook more than once before I
was done, because it was the only good water in the whole land.

Then I followed the road to the castle's village.

It was deserted. The merchants and the farmers and the artisans had all gone, leaving their doors
swinging. Only one hovel still had smoke coming out of its chimney, and inside, in the dark and the
smell, a pile of rags was stirring a bubbling pot. The pile of rags turned out to have a crone at
the far end of it.

She looked me over. "I should have thought they would send someone more ... more ..." She laughed.
I didn't like the laugh. "They've all left! A great storm is brewing in the east, my friend, and
all have fled before it!" She pressed something into my hand. "Take this and begone!"

And with a wave of her gnarled hand I was reeling backward out of her door, holding a scroll.

It was the rezrov spell: open even locked or enchanted objects. I cast gnusto on it, and my spell
book glowed, and the words wrote themselves into its pages in letters of light, and the scroll
vanished as the last word was copied. That's how gnusto works. I learned rezrov, which means
memorizing it, which means I studied it until it sat in my head like a coiled spring, ready to go.
And I walked east toward Krill's castle.

---

A word about magic, for anyone who thinks it would be fun.

A spell you've memorized is used up the moment you cast it. Gone. If you want to cast it again, you
sit down with your book and learn it again. And your head will only hold so much. Try to cram in
one spell too many, and you'll feel something slide out of the other side of your mind, and you
won't always know what it was. I learned that the hard way, more than once. A wizard is mostly a
person who is always studying.

The castle gate was locked and wrapped in chains. Rezrov, I said, and the chains flew into the air
and vanished, and the gate swung open with a blast of cold air. When I walked through, something
reached into my mind, felt around in there for a moment, and let go. Discarded, I think, as
uninteresting.

Good. Let Krill think so.

---

The castle was enormous and empty and wrong. Inside the gate I learned frotz again and cast it on
my lantern, and it flashed so bright I saw spots, then settled into a steady glow. With light, I
went up into the northwest tower and found the jewel room, emptied of its jewels, with one thing
left behind: an ornamented egg covered in little knobs and dials and buttons, the kind of puzzle
box a clever person could spend a week on.

I'm not a clever person. I cast rezrov on it. The pieces slid round by themselves, and the egg
opened, and inside, among a nest of tiny shredders and knives wired to every knob and button, lay a
scroll cut into ribbons. Whoever made that egg had meant for anybody who opened it to get nothing.

I kept the shreds anyway. I had a feeling.

East of the tower was the Hall of Mirrors, and it was the strangest place I have ever stood. The
north wall was glass, but it didn't reflect the hall. It looked into somewhere else: an underground
labyrinth of tunnels and caves and strange rock. And once, as I walked along it, a bedraggled
adventurer came into view on the other side of the glass, carrying a brass lantern and an elvish
sword that glowed faintly blue. He stopped and stared in my direction, as though he could almost
see me. Then he checked his compass and walked off.

I wondered who he was. I wondered whether, in his world, I was the strange one behind the glass.

---

Beyond the north gate lay a dark forest, and under a tree lay a crumpled scroll that somebody had
thrown away. Krebf, it said: repair willful damage. I cast it from the scroll onto my shredded
ribbons, and they flew together, slice by slice, into a whole scroll, faded but readable. Zifmia.
Summon a being to you. I copied it into my book.

In the swamp next door the air was full of frogs. I learned nitfol and cast it at them, and one of
them looked at me, and I looked at it, and it said, "Hello."

You haven't lived until a frog has told you to look under a lily pad. "Breep," it added. Under the
lily pad was a damp scroll: cleesh, the spell that turns a creature into a small amphibian. I did
wonder how many of those frogs had once been something else. They seemed happy enough. When I left,
they were discussing the politics of lily pad assignment.

Down in the castle's dungeon there was a cell, and on its walls generations of prisoners had
scratched the days of their lives. One block in the wall had its mortar picked away. Behind it was
a tunnel that those prisoners had dug, and in the tunnel, lying in the dirt where some poor soul
had dropped it, a stained scroll: exex, make things move faster. There was a silver spoon there
too. They'd thrown it away as too soft to dig with. I left it where it lay. It seemed to belong to
them.

---

In the portrait gallery I learned that some things can only be seen in the dark.

The first time I walked through, carrying my lantern, it was just a gallery of rich, dead people
whose eyes followed me as I walked. Nothing more. But that night, when I slept on the hard floor, I
dreamed of wandering in a darkened place with no light at all, surrounded by staring faces, and one
face, lit from behind, drawing me closer and closer.

So I went back, and I put down everything I owned in the hall outside, every light, and walked into
the gallery empty-handed. And in the dark, one portrait glowed orange, as if a candle were burning
behind it. Because one was. Behind the portrait was a niche, and in the niche stood a black candle
and a black scroll. Ozmoo: survive unnatural death.

I remember thinking that was a strange spell to need. I didn't know yet.

At the top of the southwest tower there was a bedroom with a feather bed in it, and it was the most
comfortable thing I touched in that whole castle. I fell asleep the moment I lay down and dreamed
of a beautiful lady standing by that same bed with a scroll in her hand, her other hand on the
bedpost, turning so I couldn't see what she was doing. When I woke I looked at the bedpost
properly. There was a tiny carved button. Inside a hidden compartment lay a gold-leaf scroll:
vaxum, make a hostile creature your friend.

I've thought about that lady often since. I hope she got away before Krill came.

---

At the top of the southeast tower was a room the old builders must have made out of pure spite. It
was full of machinery, wheels and gears and grinding things and above all a giant hammer that
smashed down on the stone floor every few seconds, and the whole of it screeched and roared like
the inside of a storm. Far across it, in a little room on the other side, something lay on the
floor that might have been a scroll. I looked at the hammer, and at the floor, and at my own soft,
unarmored self, and I went to the beach.

There was a turtle on the beach. An enormous one, crawling slowly through the dead seaweed, with a
shell enamelled in every color of the rainbow. I cast nitfol on it, and it said hello, and I asked
it to follow me. "I will follow you," it hissed, and it did, at its own leisurely pace, all the way
up the tower stairs. ("Pretty steep stairs for a turtle, friend. But if you say so...")

In the engine room I cast exex on him. He shimmered and vibrated and didn't seem to notice. Then I
shouted my orders all in one go, because I knew he'd never hear a second command over that noise:
go across, get the scroll, come back.

He went like a rainbow-colored arrow. The machinery sped up when he set foot in it, as if it could
tell, and the noise became unbearable, but he was through. Then he came back the same way with the
scroll in his mouth, and halfway across he set off a trap, and sharp spears flew at him from every
side and bounced off his shell like hail off a roof. He dodged one last crash of the hammer and
slid to a stop at my feet and dropped a brittle scroll there.

"Not bad, huh?"

Not bad at all. The scroll was kulcad, the spell that dispels other spells, and it was so long and
complicated that my book could never have held it. I would have to cast it straight from the
scroll, once. I carried it very carefully.

---

Now I have to tell you how I died.

In a closet off the south hall stood a jewelled box, tied up in thin rope wound into a thousand
loops and knots that your eyes slid off. I couldn't open it, and even rezrov couldn't open it. Some
things need a blade.

Beyond the courtyard stood Krill's temple, where the hunched and hairy shapes that served him
chanted day and night. I knew what they did to people who walked in there. I learned ozmoo, and I
ate and drank, and then I put everything I owned down on the grass of the courtyard, my book, my
bread, my jug, my candle, my scrolls, and the box. And I walked into the temple with nothing.

They took me, of course. Hundreds of them. A figure in a robe of deepest black, with a vicious
dagger in his hand, told them to take the victim to the tower while he prepared the sacrifice, and
they threw me into a cell. I cast ozmoo on myself in there, and a puff of orange smoke went up, and
I felt no different at all, and I had to just hope.

Then they came for me. They marched me up the steps to the altar, and the black figure pulled me
down onto it, and drew out a great glowing dagger, and with a murmur of approval from the crowd he
plunged it into my heart.

And I woke.

I was lying on the altar with the dagger in my chest, filled with a strange warmth, in no pain at
all. The robed figure was gone. The crowd below was still chanting and paid me no attention
whatsoever. I pulled the dagger out. There wasn't a mark on me. The blade shone faintly in the
torchlight.

I walked out of that temple with Krill's own sacrificial knife in my hand, and touched it to the
rope on the jewelled box, and the rope came alive and untangled itself into a single strand and
dissolved into the air. Inside the box was a vellum scroll. Melbor: protect magic users from harm
by evil beings.

I sat on the grass for a while after that. Being stabbed in the heart, even when it doesn't take,
is not something you just get up from.

---

The adventurer from the other side of the mirror turned out to be the most useful person I met on
my whole quest, and he never knew my name.

I walked all the way back to the Shady Brook for fresh water first, and slept by the castle gate,
and learned three spells, my head full to the brim. Then I stood in the Hall of Mirrors and waited,
watching the glass. When the bedraggled adventurer came into view, I cast zifmia at him, and he was
suddenly standing in front of me, sword in hand, jaw dropped, eyes darting about for a way out. I
cast melbor on myself, in case. Then I cast vaxum on him, and he smiled at me with an air of great
good will, and immediately asked to see what I was holding.

He followed me everywhere after that, mostly I think because of the dagger. He seemed to collect
things.

In the northeast tower stood a door like nothing I'd ever imagined. Its lock was wrapped in a dozen
iron chains. A five-headed monster with spears for tongues was embedded in its frame. Gargoyles on
either side spewed flame and ash, and ninety-seven slimy tentacles groped at me, and a sign floated
above it all, glowing purple: "Don't Bother."

I didn't. I asked the adventurer to open it.

He shrugged and walked straight up to it. Buckets of molten lead poured over his head. Twenty-seven
knives skewered him. "Did you try the doorknob?" he asked. He turned one of the gargoyle heads,
ignoring the human-sized rats feeding on his burned body, and said, "I think it's unlocked," and
walked through, and the door was just a plain wooden door. It always had been. An illusion can't
fool someone who doesn't believe in it.

Beyond it was a map room, and in the map room was an old parchment map and a pencil inscribed with
runes. I dropped the dagger. He picked it up, very pleased, and I took the map and the pencil while
he was looking at it. The last I saw of him, he was asking for directions to Flood Control Dam #3.
I hope he found it.

---

In the burned library, among the ashes, the rat tracks led to a hole in the wall, and in the hole
was a frayed scroll: gondar, quench an open flame. There was a dusty book, too, nearly destroyed.
Only two chapter titles could be read. The Legend of the Unseen Terror. The Legend of the Great
Implementers.

I knew about the Terror by then. Everyone in the Circle did. It was older than Krill, and it was
imprisoned somewhere under the castle, and Krill wanted to set it free.

Under the dungeon lay a maze of translucent rooms with cream-colored walls and perfectly round
black doorways, and the map in my hands was a map of them: nine points joined by thin pencil lines.
And here is the thing about that map. Draw a line with the rune pencil, and a passage opened
between those two rooms. Rub one out, and the passage closed. But the pencil was worn down to a
nub, and the eraser too, and I could use each one only twice.

Off in a corner of the map, connected to nothing, was a point by itself. That's where the Terror
was. And with it, sealed in, the most powerful scroll in the castle.

I drew one line. The wall opened. An evil presence came on a cold blast of air and moved beside me,
and fear covered me like a fog, and Belboz appeared before me, flickering, terrified, to say that I
must not let the Terror escape, or we were all doomed. Then he faded. My feet were lead. The first
time I tried to walk through that new doorway I ran and ran and got nowhere. The second time I made
it, and there lay the scroll, giving off power like heat from a stove.

Then I used my eraser, twice, and closed the passages behind the Terror, so that the only open path
was the one I would leave by. I drew one last line, and stepped through it, and behind me, through
the thin walls, I heard the Terror scream as it understood that it was trapped, and its scroll of
power was gone.

Guncho, the scroll said. Banish the victim to another plane of existence.

---

The way to Krill was up a winding stair that went up forever and down forever and never arrived
anywhere. I read kulcad from the brittle scroll, and the whole stair dissolved under me, the
bannister too, and I was standing in mid-air above a bottomless pit with, I'm told, the face of a
cartoon character who has just looked down. The bannister turned into a scroll in my hands as I
fell. Izyuk, it said. Fly. I cast it while I was still falling, and slowed, and floated, and
drifted east into the Warlock's Tower.

Krill turned from the horrible magic he was working. I had seen him before. He had been the figure
in black at the altar, the one who put the knife into my heart.

"Fool! Parlor magician!" His voice was as deep as caves. "You dare defile my chamber with your
worm-like presence?"

He snapped his fingers, and a dragon appeared, breathing fire. I cast gondar, and the flame drowned
in a torrent of water, and the dragon vanished with a scream. He snapped again, and a being of pure
darkness came at me with a battle axe. I cast cleesh, and it turned into a newt, and its axe fell
on it. I'm not proud of that one, but I'm not sorry either.

"I am through playing games, carnival-clown!" And Krill began to chant, a chant that shook the
whole tower.

I read the guncho scroll aloud.

He heard the first words and recoiled. For a few seconds he tried to finish his own spell first,
racing me, and he fumbled his syllables. Then he screamed and threw out his hands toward me and was
gone, banished, out of this world entirely. The tower began to sway. The floor gave way beneath me,
and I fell toward the sea, and I was sure, quite calmly sure, that this time it was over.

Then there was a burst of light.

---

I was back in the small dark chamber of the Circle. Belboz was rising from his seat. "The evil of
Krill is ended this day," he said. "From beyond hope, you have proved yourself great and worthy."

A chair appeared at his right hand, and he motioned me to sit beside him, and he smiled.

"Join with us," he said. "And tell us of your quest."

So I have. They chose me because I was nobody. It turns out nobody can get quite a lot done, with
four spells, a loaf of bread, a good jug of water, a friendly turtle, and an adventurer from
another world who doesn't believe in doors.
