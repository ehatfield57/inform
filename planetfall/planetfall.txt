PLANETFALL
Floyd and the Ensign

I was scrubbing the deck when the ship blew up.

That's the kind of Ensign I was. Ensign Seventh Class in the Stellar Patrol, aboard the S.P.S.
Feinstein, assigned to Deck Nine with a self-contained multi-purpose scrub brush and the firm
understanding that if I polished hard enough for long enough I might one day make Ensign Sixth. I'd
just finished a stretch of decking I was rather proud of when the alien ambassador from
Blow'k-bibben-Gordo ambled down the corridor, munching on what looked like an enormous stalk of
celery, and stood on it, dripping green slime, while he handed me a brochure about his planet's
major exports and asked if I'd like a game of Bocci.

Then Ensign First Class Blather swaggered in, studied my work with half-closed eyes, told me there
was a position open in the toilet-scrubbing division for an Ensign Ninth Class, gave me thirty
demerits, and glared at me with his arms crossed.

Then the ship blew up.

---

A massive explosion rocked the Feinstein. The escape-pod door beside me slid open. Blather,
confused by anything that wasn't in the regulations, ordered me to keep scrubbing and ran off. I'm
not proud of many things, but I'm proud that I didn't keep scrubbing. I went through that door into
the pod and strapped myself into the safety webbing just as the bulkhead clanged shut behind me,
and the explosions went on and on, and then there was a jolt, and the pod was falling.

It fell for a long time. When it finally hit, it hit an island in an ocean on a planet I'd never
heard of, and it began to sink at once. I grabbed the survival kit and a towel that had fallen out
of a hatch, and opened the door, and the cold sea rushed in, and I swam up out of a sinking pod
onto a rocky crag at the foot of a cliff.

Above me, carved into the cliff, was a stairway. At the top of the stairway was a courtyard. And
beyond the courtyard was a whole abandoned complex, corridors and labs and dormitories and offices,
built by people who were very clever and very orderly and who had, as far as I could see, all
completely disappeared.

---

I'll tell you about the Ensign Seventh Class part of it first, because it's mostly mops and keys.

I found a curved magnetic bar in a storage room, and held it over a crack in the floor of a damaged
hallway, and with a spray of dust and a loud clank, a key leaped out of the crevice and stuck to
it. The key fit a padlock on a storeroom door, and behind the door was an extendable ladder, and
the ladder, extended, reached across a rift that had opened in a corridor floor. I crossed it on
hands and knees with the ladder swaying, looking down at sharp pointy things, and on the other
side, in two offices, I found three cards: a kitchen access card, an upper elevator access card,
and a shuttle access card. Whoever these people had been, they liked cards.

I was hungry by then. The survival kit had three blobs of goo in it, red and green and brown. The
red one tasted just like scrumptious cherry pie. I would come to think about that goo the way
sailors think about rum rations.

---

And then I met Floyd.

In a room full of broken-down robots, there was one, about four feet high, that looked even
remotely close to working. I opened a compartment in it and found a card, of course, and closed it,
and turned it on. Nothing happened. I shrugged and walked off.

A few rooms later something came bounding up behind me.

"Floyd here now!" he cried.

Floyd was a multiple purpose robot, and he had been switched off for a very long time, and he was
delighted about everything. He followed me everywhere. When I went through a door he came bounding
after me shouting "Hey, wait for Floyd!" When I stopped he'd tell me about the time he helped
someone sharpen a pencil, or lower his voice and tell me the latest rumors about Dr. Fizpick, or
recite the first six hundred digits of pi. He asked me if I wanted to play Hucka-Bucka-Beanstalk.
He hid behind me when he saw a mouse. He went off "exploring" and came back. He fretted about his
batteries. He told me about his friend Lazarus, a medical robot, whom he hadn't seen in a long time.

I had been alone on that planet for one day, and I already couldn't remember what it had been like
before Floyd.

---

Up a tower, in an elevator that ran on one of the cards, there was a communications room, with a
send console that was broken. A flashing sign in the strange spelling those people used said
"Malfunkshun in Sendeeng Kuulint Sistum", and next to it was a panel of colored lights, and one of
them, a yellow one, was flashing, and below it was a funnel-shaped hole.

In a chemical room far below, a row of spouts and colored buttons. I put a glass flask under the
spout and pressed the yellow button, and the flask filled with yellow fluid that slowly turned
milky white. I carried it all the way back up the elevator and poured it into the funnel, and all
the lights went out except one red one. So I went back down and filled the flask red, and came back
up and poured it in, and the panel went dark and another light came on that said the help message
was being sent.

Somewhere up there, maybe, somebody was listening. I wondered if anyone on the Feinstein had made
it. I wondered if Blather had.

That night I slept in one of the dormitory bunks, with Floyd bouncing impatiently at the foot of
the bed in the morning. "About time you woke up!" I ate the green goo, which tasted just like yummy
lima beans, and filled an octagonal canteen with brown protein liquid from a machine in the
kitchen. And I noticed I felt a bit weak and slightly flushed.

---

That was the Disease.

I pieced the story together slowly, from the computer printouts and the library, from the robots
and the empty rooms. The planet was called Resida, and the people who'd built all of this had been
dying of a sickness they couldn't cure. So they'd frozen themselves. Millions of them, asleep in
cryo-units deep underground, and they'd left the Project running: robots and computers working,
generation after generation, to find a cure and wake them up when it was found. But things had
broken down over the centuries. The communications. The planetary defenses. The course control that
kept the planet's orbit steady. The great computer itself. And the Disease was in the air, still,
waiting for whoever came next.

It had me now. I didn't have long.

---

I fixed what I could. Down another elevator was a shuttle, a long transport car on rails in a
tunnel, and I slid the shuttle card through the slot and pushed the lever up and watched the
display climb: five, ten, fifteen, all the way to sixty, and then pulled it back, carefully, as the
tunnel began to rise, so that we glided into the far station and came to rest exactly at the
platform. Lawanda Station, the sign said. Floyd cheered.

In a room called Course Control, a cube full of electronics held a fused bedistor; I'd found a good
one in a storeroom, and I pulled the burnt one out with a pair of pliers and put the new one in,
and the warning lights went out.

In Planetary Defense, a panel of fromitz boards had one that was fried, and I needed a good one,
and the only good one was in a little crawlspace behind the repair room, through an opening much
too small for me.

"Floyd," I said, "go north."

He squeezed through the opening and was gone for a long time, and came back looking downcast.
"Floyd found a rubber ball inside. Lots of fun." I told him to get the shiny fromitz board. He
shrugged. "If you say so." He vanished again and came back with it. I put it in the panel and the
defense lights stopped flashing.

He was so pleased with himself. Then he said "Floyd going exploring. See you later," and glided out
of the room, and came bounding back a minute later. "Floyd here now!"

---

In the computer room, the great computer had a single light glowing on its panel. Floyd examined it
with a concerned frown.

"Uh oh," he said. "Computer is broken. A Doctor-person once told Floyd that Computer is the most
important thing in the Project."

The computer's printout said the cure was all but finished: preliminary research complete,
intermediate research complete, final research complete, drug production complete. Drug testing was
99.985 percent done. It had stopped, just short. Something inside the computer itself, in sector
384, was broken. And there was a booth beside the computer room for shrinking down a repairman and
sending him inside the machine. But the card that ran it was in the Bio Lab.

I looked through the Bio Lab window and wished I hadn't. The experiments had got loose, generations
ago, and bred, and changed. There was a creature with a hairy shell and a whip-like tail. A giant
plant that walked, teeming with poisonous tentacles. Something with slavering fangs. A deformed
thing that had once, maybe, been a man. And on the floor, among them, the card I needed.

Floyd stood on his tiptoes and looked in through the window. "Looks dangerous in there," he said.
And then, after a moment: "We'll need card there to fix computer. Hmmm... I know! Floyd will get
card. Robots are tough. Nothing can hurt robots. You open the door, then Floyd will rush in. Then
you close door. When Floyd knocks, open door again."

His voice trembled slightly while he waited.

I opened the door. Floyd, pausing only for the briefest moment, plunged in. I closed it. From
inside I heard ferocious growlings, and the sounds of a fight, and things breaking. I stood there
with my hand on the door. And then, slightly muffled, three fast knocks.

I opened the door. Floyd stumbled out, clutching the card, and I slammed the door shut, and not a
moment too soon: the monsters pounded on the other side of it as if they'd break it down.

Floyd staggered, and fell, and dropped the card. He was badly torn apart. I knelt down on the floor
of that bio-lock and held his head in my lap, and he looked up at me with half-open eyes. "Floyd
did it ... got card," he said. "Floyd a good friend, huh?" So I sang him his favorite song,
quietly, the old spacer's ballad about the Starcrossed Miner, who went aboard the alien ship out
beyond the orb of Mars, and solved its mysteries, and found the lode. Floyd's eyes closed. And the
oil spread out underneath him on the floor.

I don't mind telling you that I cried. I'm Patrol. Patrol doesn't cry. I did anyway.

---

Then I picked up the card, because it had cost him everything, and I went and used it.

In the miniaturization booth I slid it through the slot and typed 384, and the walls slid away in
every direction, or I shrank, I couldn't tell which, and I was standing on a plate of metal inside
the computer, on a silicon strip over a bottomless void. In a micro-relay encased in red plastic, a
single speck of dirt had wedged itself into the works, and that speck had stopped a whole world
from waking up. I set the laser to its finest beam and fired at it. I missed. I missed again. The
laser got warm. I hit it, and it sizzled. And on the seventh shot it vaporized into a fine cloud of
dust.

On the way back a microbe came for me: towering, transparent, cilia waving, pseudo-pods reaching. I
turned the laser up and fired into it, again and again, and it sealed itself up every time, and
kept coming, and its pseudo-pods kept reaching not for me but for the laser, which was getting
hotter and hotter, as if it liked the warmth. When the laser was very warm, I threw it over the
edge of the strip into the void. The microbe lunged after it, and was gone.

The main booth had broken, it said in my ear, and sent me to an auxiliary booth beside the Bio Lab
instead.

In the lab office was a desk, and in the desk was a gas mask, and on the wall was a red button. I
put on the mask and pushed the button, and a mist began hissing into the Bio Lab. I opened the door
and walked in among the monsters, and they were groggy from the gas, and I walked straight through
them and out the other side, and then the gas wore off and they came after me.

I ran. Through the bio-lock, past Floyd lying where I'd left him. Through the Main Lab, the mutants
right on my heels, needle-sharp mandibles nipping at me. Through the corridor. Into the office
where, on the south wall, a garish mural had hung for centuries, and was gone now: behind it was
the elevator the computer had opened for the revival. I fell into it and hit the one button, and
the door closed just as the monsters reached it.

---

It went down a long way. When the door opened, I was in a cold little room beside a vast hall of
cryo-units, thousands upon thousands of them, chamber after chamber, millions of sleepers. A
medical robot glided in, gave an injection to the sleeper in the one cryo-unit beside me, and a
woman rose from it. Middle-aged, with flowing red hair. She studied the panel for a while, and
pressed some keys, and all through the chambers beyond, cryo-units began to open.

"I am Veldina, leader of Resida," she said, in a beautiful lilting voice. "Thanks to you, the cure
has been discovered, and the planetary systems repaired. We are eternally grateful."

The Patrol had heard my message after all. A ship was in orbit, the S.P.S. Flathead, and a landing
party materialized in the room as if on cue, and there was Blather with them, picked up from deep
space in another escape pod, babbling. Captain Sterling of the Flathead shook my hand and told me I
was promoted to Lieutenant First Class. Veldina told me the grateful people of Resida were offering
me the leadership of their world. Captain Sterling added that, even if I chose to stay, Blather
(now Ensign Twelfth Class) had been assigned as my personal toilet attendant. And a medical robot
stung my arm, and the Disease went out of me.

And then a team of robot technicians filed into the room, and parted their ranks, and something
came bounding toward me.

"Hi!" shouted Floyd, with uncontrolled enthusiasm. "Floyd feeling better now!"

He was patched and dented and polished and grinning from ear to ear. "Look what Floyd found!" he
said, and handed me a helicopter key, a reactor elevator card, and a paddle-ball set. "Maybe we can
use them in the sequel..."

I don't know yet whether I'll stay on Resida. But wherever I go, I already know who's coming with
me.
